# Alerts by text message (design, OFF)

Status 2026-10-31: code complete, switched off. Nothing sends a text: `SMS_ALERTS_ENABLED` is unset and, without `LINQ_API_KEY`, there is no sender at all. Turning it on is the owner's call (GOAL.md: no paid call, no deploy without a yes); the steps are at the end.

## What it is

Opt-in: a traveller adds a phone number in Settings, proves it with a six digit code, and from then on the alerts that change what they do next also arrive as an iMessage or SMS from the Soma Linq line, next to the push. Same alert, same copy, same per-category switches. Push stays the main channel; texts are for people who keep notifications off or travel with a second phone.

## Two modes, one number

- **Text Me Too** (`sms_alerts.enabled`): the alerts that change the next step (`SMS_KINDS`: departure delay of 30 minutes or more, gate change, cancellation, diversion, connection at risk), next to the push.
- **In-Flight Texts** (`sms_alerts.in_flight`, or the flight sheet's More menu, "Text Me Updates In Flight", stored per follow in `sms_flight_prefs`): for airline Wi-Fi that only lets messaging apps through (the free iMessage and WhatsApp tiers). Between boarding (45 minutes before the best departure, or the push-back) and 30 minutes after arrival at the gate (or 3 hours after the best arrival while the gate is not reached), the alerts that matter on board (`IN_FLIGHT_KINDS`): arrival delay of 15 minutes or more, diversion, arrival gate and terminal (assigned or changed), baggage belt, connection at risk, and the landing, whose text carries the onward connection: "Next: AF 85 from gate K40 at 3:10 PM, 55m to connect, tight." (the Connection Assistant's verdict and the onward gate). A flight's switch wins over the account mode both ways; switching it back to the account's value clears it.

Every text is "Soma Flight: <title>. <body> Reply STOP to stop.", under 300 characters. The per-category switches in Settings > Notifications apply to texts too.

## Who gets what, once

push-sender claims each alert once in `alert_log` (a retry never sends it twice), then calls `smsHook` for it with the flight. The hook returns before any read while the flag is off or there is no sender. Otherwise it reads the followers' rows that have either mode on, their flight switches when the alert is an on-board one in the window, and plans one text per user, whichever mode asked (`planSms`, pure). Before each send, `claim_sms(user, flight, alert key)` inserts a row in `sms_log`: an alert both modes want, or a second push-sender call, is texted once. The same function caps an account at 10 texts an hour and 30 a day. Linq also gets an idempotency key (flight, user, alert), so its own retry cannot double a text. A failing text never stops the push (the hook's errors are caught in `deliver`).

## What is built

- `supabase/migrations/20261031000024_sms_alerts.sql`: one row per account (`sms_alerts`): E.164 number, code hash and expiry, tries, `verified_at`, `enabled`, `opted_out_at`. Checks: E.164 only; a code has its expiry; `enabled` needs a verified number and no STOP; a number is verified for one account only. RLS: owners read their own row (never the hash) and may delete it; every other write is service role. Account deletion cascades. pgTAP: `supabase/tests/sms_alerts_test.sql`.
- `supabase/migrations/20261031000028_sms_in_flight.sql`: `sms_alerts.in_flight` (same rule as `enabled`), `sms_flight_prefs` (per follow, cascades on unfollow, owner read), `sms_log` and `claim_sms` (dedupe and caps), `sms_code_log` and `sms_code_allowed` (3 codes an hour per account and per number, the number kept only as a peppered SHA-256), `sms_inbound_events` (webhook dedupe). Functions are service role only. pgTAP: `supabase/tests/sms_in_flight_test.sql`.
- `supabase/functions/_shared/sms_alerts.ts`: the flag (only the exact string "true" is on), `normalisePhone`, `newCode` (CSPRNG), `hashCode`, `checkCode` (10 minutes, 5 tries), `canReach`, `canText`, `wantsSms`, `wantsInFlight`, `inFlightWindow`, `smsText`, `planSms`, `onwardLine`, the `SmsSender` interface and `smsHook`. Tests: `sms_alerts_test.ts`, `sms_in_flight_test.ts` (fake sender, in-memory database `sms_test_db.ts`).
- `supabase/functions/_shared/linq.ts`: `LinqSender implements SmsSender`, written against Linq's public Partner API v3 docs (docs.linqapp.com, read 2026-10-31): `POST https://api.linqapp.com/api/partner/v3/chats` with `from` (our line), `to` and one text part, bearer `LINQ_API_KEY`, `message.idempotency_key`; Linq picks iMessage, then RCS, then SMS per recipient. One retry on 429, 5xx or a network failure. Refuses without `LINQ_API_KEY` or `LINQ_FROM_NUMBER`. Logs carry the status, Linq's error code and trace id, never the number or the words. Also the Standard Webhooks signature check (`webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp`, `webhook-signature`, HMAC-SHA256 with the `whsec_` secret, 5 minutes) and the `message.received` reader (both webhook versions). Tests: `linq_test.ts` (fake fetch).
- `supabase/functions/sms-verify` (signed in): `status` (the row and a flight's switch), `start` (normalise, refuse a number verified elsewhere, 3 codes an hour, new hash and expiry, the code by text), `check` (wrong tries counted, verified sets Text Me Too on and clears a past STOP), `settings` (the two switches, verified numbers only), `flight` (the per flight switch, followed flights only), `forget` (deletes the row; always allowed). Everything but `status` and `forget` answers 503 while the flag is off. Tests: `sms-verify/index_test.ts`.
- `supabase/functions/sms-inbound` (Linq's webhook, `verify_jwt = false`, signature checked): only `message.received` from a number in `sms_alerts`, only carrier keywords (the line is shared with the Soma companion, whose conversations pass through untouched). STOP, STOPALL, UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL, END, QUIT: `opted_out_at`, both modes off, one reply. HELP, INFO: the support link. START, UNSTOP, YES: a reply pointing to the app (texts come back only through a new verification). Each event id once. Tests: `sms-inbound/index_test.ts`.
- `push-sender`: `deliver` calls the hook with the flight, the clock and a lazy onward connection read (only for an in-flight landing text). `index.ts` wires `sms: { db, sender: linqFromEnv() }`: without `LINQ_API_KEY` the sender is undefined, and without the flag the hook returns at once.
- App (FlightKit): Settings > Notifications > **Text Me Too** (`TextMeTooSection`, `TextAlertsStore`): number, code, the Text Me Too and In-Flight Texts switches, Forget My Number, and the line "Your number is used for flight alerts only." While the backend has texts off the section reads "Coming soon". The flight sheet's More menu shows **Text Me Updates In Flight** (`FlightTextsModel`) once a number is verified. Services: `SupabaseTextAlertsService` (`sms-verify`), `FakeTextAlertsService` for `-useFakes` (code 123456, nothing sent).

## Offline and in-flight mode in the app (works today, no server)

When the device has no connection (`Connectivity`, `NWPathMonitor`), or a flight in the air has had no update for 5 minutes, `FlightEstimate` works out on the device where the flight probably is: the share of the best gate-to-gate time elapsed, placed on the great circle between the two airports (the Rust core's `greatCircle`). Offline, a flight whose departure time passed counts as flying. The flight sheet shows "Estimated, last update 14:02" with a dashed progress bar under the freshness line, the globe moves the plane along the arc once a minute with a dashed ring, and the home shows a calm grey banner, "No connection: showing your saved flights". My Flights, Passport, boarding passes and the flight sheet read from the on-device cache as before. The Live Activity advances by time too (the extension draws progress at render time from the last state's times, and the app updates it every 5 minutes while the flight is in the air), like the widgets' timeline already did. `-useFakes -fakeOffline` shows the offline state for screenshots.

## Turning texts on (owner steps, in order)

1. **Linq plan and cost.** Linq does not publish prices; competitors' comparisons put a dedicated line from about $250 a month (flat per line, not per message), quoted by a Linq representative. The free plan on today's line allows 20 contacts and the line is shared with the Soma companion: ask Linq for a second line dedicated to flight alerts (a separate number keeps STOP for alerts apart from the companion's conversations) and an API token for it. Read Linq's terms for app notifications (A2P use) before signing.
2. **A2P registration if SMS is used.** Linq sends iMessage first, then RCS, then SMS. For the SMS fallback to US numbers the line needs carrier registration: 10DLC (brand plus a "notifications" campaign with the opt-in flow, sample messages, STOP and HELP texts from `sms-inbound/handler.ts`) for a local number, or toll-free verification for a toll-free one. Ask Linq which applies to the line and let them file it; allow one to three weeks.
3. **Secrets** (Supabase project `soma-flight`, Edge Functions secrets): `LINQ_API_KEY` (the token), `LINQ_FROM_NUMBER` (the line in E.164), `LINQ_WEBHOOK_SECRET` (the `whsec_` secret of the webhook subscription), `SMS_CODE_PEPPER` (32 random bytes, for example `openssl rand -hex 32`; never change it while codes are pending).
4. **Deploy and webhook.** `supabase db push` (migration 20261031000028), `supabase functions deploy sms-verify sms-inbound push-sender`, then create a Linq webhook subscription for `message.received` on the line, pointing at `https://<project>.supabase.co/functions/v1/sms-inbound`.
5. **Privacy.** App Store privacy label: add Phone Number (App Functionality, linked to the user, not used for tracking), and the same in `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` (`NSPrivacyCollectedDataTypePhoneNumber`). Privacy policy line: "If you turn on Text Me Too, we keep your mobile number to text you flight alerts, through our messaging provider Linq. It is used for nothing else, never shared or sold, and deleted when you tap Forget My Number, reply STOP (texts stop at once), or delete your account."
6. **The switch.** Set `SMS_ALERTS_ENABLED=true`. The app's section changes from "Coming soon" to the number field on its next load. Test with the owner's own number first: verify, turn In-Flight Texts on, follow a flight in the air, reply HELP and STOP.

To turn it off again, unset `SMS_ALERTS_ENABLED` (nothing sends, numbers stay) or remove `LINQ_API_KEY` (no sender).

## Out of scope

Answering questions by text ("is my flight on time?"), which would route through the companion with AeroAPI cache reads: possible later, not part of alerts.
